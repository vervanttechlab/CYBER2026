SLIDE 8 — THE ALERT PIPELINE
Title: "The Alert Pipeline"
Horizontal 5-step flow with icons:
1. SENSORS — AV, firewall, WAF, DLP, EDR, NDR. Each one sees a slice.
2. SIEM — collects, correlates, ranks.
3. ANALYST — you. The SIEM says "interesting." You decide if it is real.
4. ACTION — a ticket, an escalation, a phone call at 2 AM.
5. RECORD — what you did and why.

SLIDE 10 — THE SHIFT HANDOVER
Title: "The Shift Handover — Five Sections"
Five labelled items:
- OPEN ITEMS — tickets in progress, with number and state

- WATCH ITEMS — not yet incidents, but they need eyes

Add the line: "The handover is the highest-risk moment in a SOC. It is where information gets dropped."
Visual: five stacked bars or a clipboard infographic.

SLIDE 11 — THE READ-BACK RULE
Title: "The Read-Back Rule"
Headline: "Repeat the instruction in your own words before you act on it."
Example quote: "So you want me to isolate WKS-042 — not WKS-024 — and notify the IT manager. Confirming."
A good read-back has three parts:
1. The identifier, said back exactly — hostname, IP, ticket number
2. The action you are being asked to take
3. The notification — who you will tell
Add the line: "It takes four seconds. Pilots do it. Nurses do it."
Visual: a two-way arrow between two speech bubbles.

SLIDE 12 — WHEN TO STOP AND ASK
Title: "When to Stop and Ask"
Three situations mean stop and seek clarification:
- You do not understand the instruction
- The instruction contradicts the SOP
- Acting on it would exceed your authority
Four questions you always have:
- "Can you repeat the hostname, IP, or ticket number?"
- "Now, or after I finish the current ticket?"
- "That is above my authority — escalate, or are you authorising it?"
- "The SOP says X and you are asking for Y. Which do I follow?"
Add the line, bold: "Asking is not weakness. Not asking is the error."
Visual: a raised-hand icon and four question cards.

SLIDE 13 — THE SHIFT HUDDLE
Title: "The Shift Huddle — 4 Minutes, On the Clock"
Four steps:
1. Shift Lead opens and states the priorities.
2. Each member reports open items — one sentence each.
3. The team agrees who takes the watch item, by name.
4. Shift Lead closes with the one thing that cannot slip tonight.
Add the line: "Everyone speaks, every time. The timer keeps it honest."
Visual: a circular 4-step flow with a stopwatch in the centre.

SLIDE 17 — READING AN SOP
Title: "Reading an SOP: Watch the Verbs"
Table with two columns (Word, What it means for you):
- shall / must | Mandatory. You have no discretion.
- should | Expected. If you deviate, you must justify it.
- may | Your judgement — usually conditional on documenting it.
- shall not | You have no authority. This is the edge of your role.
- within X minutes | A clock is running. Know when it starts.
Visual: a highlighter-pen motif over a document. Keep as a table.

SLIDE 18 — ADVISORY VS CHANGE NOTICE
Title: "Advisory vs Change Notice"
Two-column comparison:
- ADVISORY: Tells you a threat, patch, or vulnerability exists. Usually informational, sometimes actionable.
- CHANGE NOTICE: Tells you a system will be modified at a set time. Always relevant to your queue.
Add the line: "Scheduled change is the number one cause of false alerts. A notice tells you what is NOT covered just as much as what is."
Visual: two contrasting document cards side by side.
