AI PRESENTATION BUILDER PROMPT
Cyber Threat Monitoring Level I (15-Day Program) — Day 01
AFTERNOON — Self-Study Session (1:00 to 5:00 PM)
============================================================

HOW TO USE:
1. Copy the prompt below (from "Create a modern..." to the end).
2. Paste it into your AI presentation builder (Gamma, Canva AI, SlidesAI, etc.).
3. Generate the slides, then export as 16:9 widescreen via Canva.
4. This deck is intentionally short: 12 slides.

WHAT THIS DECK IS FOR:
The afternoon is asynchronous — you are not on the call. Trainees work alone from
1:00 to 5:00. Send them this deck together with the Activity Pack.

They open the deck, and it walks them through the eight tasks one slide at a time.
It replaces you standing at the front. Every slide tells them what to do, how long
it should take, and what to hand in.

You show slides 1, 2 and 3 on the morning call at 11:50 so they know what is coming,
then send the whole deck at lunch.

COLOR SCHEME:
- Primary dark blue: #1B3A5C
- Accent blue: #2E75B6
- Accent teal: #009688
- Light background: #D6E4F0
- White backgrounds with blue accents, sans-serif fonts.

PROMPT (12 SLIDES) — COPY EVERYTHING BELOW
============================================================

Create a modern, professional training presentation of EXACTLY 12 slides. Use a navy and white color scheme (#1B3A5C navy, #2E75B6 accent blue, #009688 teal, white background). Use clean sans-serif fonts. Every slide must include a visual element — icons, simple infographics, or diagrams. Do NOT use stock photos of hackers, hoodies, padlocks, or binary code. Format 16:9 widescreen. Keep text short and readable.

IMPORTANT: This deck is read by a student working ALONE, with no teacher present. So write every slide as a direct instruction to that student. Use "you" throughout. Every task slide must show: what to do, how long it takes, and what to hand in. Audience is adult vocational trainees in the Philippines with mixed technical background. Keep the tone plain, warm and encouraging — they are on their own and some of them are nervous.

SLIDE 1 — TITLE
Title: "Day 01 — Afternoon"
Subtitle: "Your Self-Study Session · 1:00 to 5:00 PM"
Kicker: "Eight tasks · About 3 hours 40 minutes · Work at your own pace"
Visual: a calm navy title slide with a desk-and-notebook illustration.

SLIDE 2 — HOW THIS AFTERNOON WORKS
Title: "You Are Working On Your Own Now"
Four short points with icons:
- Eight tasks. About 3 hours 40 minutes of work, in a 4-hour window.
- Do them in any order — EXCEPT read the handout (Task 4) before Tasks 5, 6 and 8.
- Everything you produce goes in your portfolio. It is marked.
- Stuck? Message your trainer. Do not sit stuck for an hour.
Add the line: "This is not a half day. It is the second half of Day 1, and it covers a part of the qualification the morning did not."
Visual: a simple 8-step progress tracker.

SLIDE 3 — YOUR EIGHT TASKS
Title: "Your Eight Tasks"
Table with three columns (Task, Time, Hand in):
1. Take the pre-test | 45 min | Submitted automatically
2. Set up your evidence folder | 15 min | A screenshot
3. Write up this morning's handover | 20 min | Shift Handover Log
4. Read the Student Handout | 30 min | Nothing to hand in
5. Mark up the Alert Intake SOP | 45 min | Worksheet + 4 answers
6. Triage three written notices | 30 min | Grid + 3 answers
7. Evaluate a security advisory | 25 min | Evaluation sheet
8. Reflection and Day 2 prep | 10 min | Short answers
Highlight rows 5 and 6.
Add the line: "Tasks 5 and 6 matter most. If you run short of time, those two cannot slip."
Visual: keep as a clean table with a blue header row.

SLIDE 4 — TASK 1: THE PRE-TEST
Title: "Task 1 — Take the Pre-Test (45 minutes)"
Instructions:
- Open the link your trainer sent you
- Sign in with your course Google account
- You get ONE attempt
- Do not switch tabs once you start — the test records it
Big highlighted box: "DO NOT LOOK ANYTHING UP."
Add the line: "This does not affect your result. It tells your trainer where to spend the next 15 days. Most people score low — that is exactly what a pre-test is for. An honest low score helps you. A researched high score helps nobody."
Visual: a checklist or quiz icon. Keep it reassuring, not exam-like.

SLIDE 5 — TASK 2: YOUR EVIDENCE FOLDER
Title: "Task 2 — Set Up Your Evidence Folder (15 minutes)"
Show this folder structure as a diagram:
  Desktop / CTM_Level1 /
      Evidence / Day_01 ... Day_15
      Worksheets
      Tools
      Scans
Instructions:
- Right-click the Desktop, New, Folder
- Use Day_01 with a zero, not Day_1
Add the line: "Windows sorts by text, not numbers. Without the zero, Day_10 sorts before Day_2. Small habit, saves you fifteen days of mess."
Hand in: a screenshot of the folder.
Visual: a folder-tree diagram.

SLIDE 6 — TASK 3: WRITE UP THIS MORNING'S HANDOVER
Title: "Task 3 — Write Up the Handover (20 minutes)"
Instructions:
- Use the notes YOU took this morning. Not a recording. Not a classmate's notes.
- Fill in all five sections: Open items, Escalated, Watch items, System status, Client notes
Big highlighted box: "Missed something? Write down that you missed it."
Example to show on the slide:
  "Watch item: connection attempts to — could not hear the hostname clearly.
   Would confirm with the outgoing shift before acting."
Add the line: "An honest gap scores BETTER than a filled-in guess. In this job a guessed machine name sends the next shift to the wrong computer. Your trainer is marking whether you can be honest about what you do not know."
Visual: a partly-filled form with one blank field circled in teal.

SLIDE 7 — TASK 4: READ THE HANDOUT
Title: "Task 4 — Read the Student Handout (30 minutes)"
Read properly, not a skim. Pay attention to:
- What each sensor sees — AV, firewall, WAF, DLP, EDR, NDR
- A real shift, hour by hour
- The RACI grid — who owns an alert at each stage
- Who is NOT allowed to decide containment
Add the line: "Tasks 5, 6 and 8 assume you have read this. Thirty quiet minutes, no computer needed."
Visual: a reading icon and a simple four-item checklist.

SLIDE 8 — TASK 5: MARK UP THE SOP (PART 1)
Title: "Task 5 — Mark Up the SOP (45 minutes)"
Subtitle: "This is the most important task of the afternoon"
Step 1 — Open Document 1 in your SOP Pack (SOP-SOC-001) and highlight every one of these words:
Show as a table (Word / What it means for you):
  shall / must | Mandatory. No choice.
  should | Expected. If you skip it, you must justify why.
  may | Your judgement — usually only if you document it.
  shall not | You have no authority. This is the edge of your role.
  within X minutes | A clock is running. Know when it starts.
Step 2 — Fill in the worksheet table for all 8 steps.
Visual: a highlighter pen over a document.

SLIDE 9 — TASK 5: THE FOUR QUESTIONS
Title: "Task 5 — Then Answer These Four"
List the four questions:
1. An alert arrives at 22:00. You assess it as High at 22:20. What is your notification deadline, and why?
2. You close a Low alert without writing anything down. Which step did you breach?
3. A client phones and asks you to disconnect an infected laptop. What do you do, and which step says so?
4. A Change Notice explains an alert so you close it. What must you record first?
Big highlighted box: "Question 1 is about a clock. Almost everybody gets it wrong. Read section 3.3 twice."
Add the line: "Do not skip it if you are unsure — write your answer anyway. We open Day 2 with this question."
Visual: four numbered question cards.

SLIDE 10 — TASK 6: TRIAGE THREE NOTICES
Title: "Task 6 — Triage Three Written Notices (30 minutes)"
Read Documents 4, 5 and 6 in your SOP Pack. For each one answer:
1. What am I being told?
2. What must I do, and by when?
3. Which systems does it affect — and which does it NOT?
4. What if this conflicted with the SOP?
Then three scenario questions:
A. 23:30, 22 April. SRV-MNL-01 to 04 go offline. Incident or not?
B. Same night. SRV-CEB-02 goes offline. Incident or not?
C. The Northwind notice contradicts the SOP. Which do you follow?
Add the line: "Question 3 is doing more work than it looks. A notice tells you what it does NOT cover just as clearly as what it does."
Visual: three document cards side by side.

SLIDE 11 — TASKS 7 AND 8
Title: "Task 7 and Task 8 — Finishing Up"
Two columns.
LEFT — TASK 7: EVALUATE AN ADVISORY (25 min)
- Find a real security advisory published in the last 30 days
- Try a vendor page, a national CERT, or the CVE database
- Fill in the evaluation sheet
- Rank your source: Authoritative, Credible, or Unverified
- "Picking a news article and correctly ranking it Unverified is better work than picking a vendor advisory you cannot explain."
RIGHT — TASK 8: REFLECTION (10 min)
- What did you miss in this morning's handover?
- Did you speak in the huddle?
- Which task took longest, and where exactly did you get stuck?
- One thing you want explained again on Day 2
- "Be specific. 'The SOP task was hard' tells your trainer nothing."
Visual: two cards side by side.

SLIDE 12 — HANDING IN AND WHAT IS NEXT
Title: "Before You Finish"
Checklist with tick boxes:
- Pre-test submitted — I saw the confirmation screen
- Screenshot of my CTM_Level1 folder
- Shift Handover Log — from my own notes, gaps included
- Student Handout read
- SOP worksheet + 4 answers
- Written notice grid + 3 answers
- Source evaluation sheet
- Reflection answers
- This morning's Home Workstation Audit and Huddle Observation Checklist
Then: "Save everything in Evidence/Day_01 AND send copies to your trainer."
Closing line: "Tomorrow is problem solving — real faults on a real desk. Bring your marked-up SOP and your answer to Question 1. Most of you will have it wrong. That is genuinely the plan."
Visual: a completed checklist and a small Day 1 of 15 progress bar.
