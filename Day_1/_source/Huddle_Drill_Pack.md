# PART 1 — WHAT A HUDDLE IS

## The short version

**A huddle is a very short team meeting at the start of a shift. Four minutes. Everyone speaks once. Then you go to work.**

That is it. There is nothing else to it.

## Where you have already seen one

You have probably been in one without knowing the name.

- A restaurant supervisor gathers the crew before the dinner rush: "Two people out sick tonight, function room is booked at seven, air-con in section B is broken. Go."
- A call centre team leader before a shift: "System maintenance at 2am, do not promise callbacks after midnight."
- A construction foreman at the start of the day, or a security guard shift change at a mall.

Same thing. Different industry.

## What it is NOT

| It is not | Because |
|-----------|---------|
| A presentation | Nobody has slides. Nobody prepares anything |
| A discussion | You do not solve problems in a huddle. You find out what the problems are |
| A status report to the boss | It is the team telling each other, not one person reporting upward |
| Long | Four minutes. If it runs long, something has gone wrong |

If somebody starts explaining how they are going to fix something, the Shift Lead stops them: *"Take that offline."* That means "discuss it after the huddle, with the people who need to be in it."

## Why a security team does this

You are taking over a shift from people who are going home. Things are already in progress. Some of them are urgent and some are not, and you have eight hours ahead of you.

If nobody says out loud what matters most, four people spend the night working on four different things, and the important one gets missed. The huddle is four minutes of insurance against that.

# PART 2 — HOW THE DRILL RUNS

## Your team gets a scenario card

It shows the state of your shift: three open tickets, one watch item, system status, and a client note. This is what the previous shift left you.

**You get two minutes to read it.** That is all the preparation there is — a huddle is not something you rehearse.

## Then you run your huddle in four steps

| Step | Who | What they say |
|------|-----|--------------|
| **1. Open** | Shift Lead | What matters most tonight, and why |
| **2. Report** | Every member | Your open items — **one sentence each** |
| **3. Assign** | The team | Who takes the watch item — **by name** |
| **4. Close** | Shift Lead | The single thing that cannot slip tonight |

**Four minutes on the clock.** Your trainer will stop you at four minutes even if you are mid-sentence. That is part of it.

## What is being marked

Another team watches you and fills in the Observation Checklist. Your trainer is watching too.

**The one that matters most: did every single member speak?** This unit is about contributing to a group. If you say nothing, there is nothing to record — and the assessor cannot mark what did not happen.

> **Cameras on.** Both for the team running the huddle and the teams watching. This is one of the two moments today where it genuinely matters.

# PART 3 — WHAT A GOOD ONE SOUNDS LIKE

Read this before your turn. This is a full huddle, start to finish, on a different scenario from yours.

---

**SHIFT LEAD (Ana):**
> "Right, huddle. Four minutes. Top priority tonight is ticket 4471, the PE infection on WKS-042 — that scan has to be confirmed before midnight. Second is the phishing follow-up on 4478. Everything else is normal queue. Marco, open items?"

**MARCO:**
> "Two open. 4471, the PE infection — quarantine held, full scan is at sixty percent, I will confirm by eleven. 4478, phishing in Finance, three users clicked — I still need to reach them to find out whether anyone entered a password. That is it."

**ANA:**
> "Bles?"

**BLES:**
> "One open. 4482, the firewall alert storm from 203.0.113.45. I assessed it as routine scanning, it is documented, I will close it if it does not come back tonight. Nothing else."

**ANA:**
> "Ken?"

**KEN:**
> "Nothing open. I picked up the queue backlog from the day shift, about twenty low-severity items, I am working through those."

**ANA:**
> "Okay. The watch item is the Cebu firewall dropping its log feed. Ken, you have capacity — can you take that? If it stops completely, not just gaps, tell me straight away."

**KEN:**
> "Got it. Cebu firewall log feed, I tell you the moment it stops completely."

**ANA:**
> "Good. So — Marco has 4471 and 4478. Bles has 4482. Ken has the backlog and the Cebu watch item. Nobody touches 4469, that is with Level Two and Ryan owns it. No scans tonight, the scanner is in maintenance until six.
>
> If it gets busy, 4471 is the one that cannot slip. Go."

---

## Four things to notice in that example

1. **Ana opened with priorities, not with a greeting.** No "how is everyone doing." Four minutes.
2. **Every person spoke, including Ken, who had nothing open.** "Nothing open, here is what I am doing" is a valid report. Silence is not.
3. **Ken repeated the watch item back.** That is the read-back rule from earlier this morning, used without anyone mentioning it.
4. **Ana closed by naming who owns what.** Not "someone should look at Cebu." **Ken** looks at Cebu.

> **Somebody is not a person. Nobody is called Somebody.**

# PART 6 — IF YOU ARE THE SHIFT LEAD

You are running it. Four things, and you can read these off the page while you do it.

**1. Open with the priority, not a greeting.**
> "Right, huddle. Four minutes. Top priority tonight is [ticket], because [one reason]."

**2. Call each person by name.** Do not say "anyone else?" — you will get silence. Say "Marco, open items?"

**3. If somebody starts explaining how they will fix something, stop them.**
> "Take that offline — who has the next one?"

**4. Close by naming who owns what.**
> "So Marco has [X]. Bles has [Y]. Ken has the watch item. If it gets busy, [Z] is the one that cannot slip. Go."

> **The most common mistake:** letting the watch item end as "somebody should check that." Assign it to a person, out loud, by name. That single habit is what separates the teams that pass this drill from the teams that do not.
